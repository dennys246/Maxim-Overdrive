| O12 | **Processing speed is a per-NPC trait, in world time (2026-11-16).** People differ in speed, but that variation belongs to the person: stable, and independent of whose machine hosts the world. Hardware lateness is not that (one host's GPU slows every NPC at once, and most in busy scenes). Each NPC draws an onset multiplier at creation, saved with the agent, from the NPC's keyed stream (§3.4); it scales the modelled onset under either latency policy and never reads the hardware. Its distribution lives in `data/agents/`, validated at load: a lognormal, median 1 at a stated reference age, sigma 0.15 within an age (plausible 0.10–0.25; choice-RT CVs of 0.13–0.15 in age-matched cohorts, `research/speed/individual_differences.md`, *verified* there). Fatigue (§2.3) and per-response noise still vary speed within a person, and per trial that noise is larger than the gap between people. The refinements the review proposes (age, domains, turn gaps) are §12.1 item 5, for the owner. | owner, 2026-11-16 | §4.2 | live |
| O11a | **O11 amended after Maxim's response (2026-11-16, `reviews/maxim_response_1/`).** Floor-holding runs in three steps: a content-free filler, then an account ("let me think") at about 600–700 ms, then a yield at about 1 s. The order rests on the preference for progressivity: a non-answer such as an account is preferred over silence (Stivers & Robinson 2006, *verified* there; it gives no timings). The timings come from elsewhere: gaps past about 700 ms read as dispreferred (Kendrick & Torreira 2015, *verified*) and about 1 s is the "standard maximum" silence (Jefferson 1989, *to verify*). Every threshold is a per-world parameter in world time. The first filler is chosen from the **modelled** predicted delay; what follows may track real lateness, logged, tier 3, and counted by a meter of lateness-caused gaps per machine, because listeners hear long gaps as reluctance (BD-4: Kendrick & Torreira 2015, Roberts et al. 2006).
