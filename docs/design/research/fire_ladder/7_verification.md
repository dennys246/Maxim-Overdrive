# 7. Verification of claims in the ladder research notes

Checked 2026-11-20 by an independent fact-check pass. I read each claim's source myself and did not rely on the notes' wording. Downloaded and computed artefacts:
- MaCFP `matl-db`, shallow clone of `main` on that date.
- FDS manuals, `firemodels/fds` @ `5458d6ec` (2026-11-20).
- FDS outputs from `firemodels/out` (current version and its git history).
- NIST TN 2102, NBSIR 84-2943, Treede 1995 (PubMed abstract), Finney 2015 (PMC full text), Murray et al. 2014 (eScholarship author manuscript), and Emberley et al. 2017 (Edinburgh repository PDF).

Verdicts: **CONFIRMED**, **CONFIRMED-WITH-CORRECTION** (the numbers hold, but the attribution or framing is wrong), **PARTLY**, **WRONG** or **UNVERIFIABLE**.

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emberley 2017: timber self-extinguishes below ~45 ± 1 kW/m² at a critical MLR ~3.7 g/m²s | **CONFIRMED-WITH-CORRECTION** | The numbers are real, but they come from **a different Emberley 2017 paper and apply to one product only**. The source is Emberley, Gorska Putynska, … Torero, "Description of small and large-scale cross laminated timber fire tests", *FSJ* 91:327–335. Its small-scale section tests **Radiata Pine CLT** (150 mm, 27 samples, cone heater) and says: "The critical mass loss rate for flame extinction was measured as 3.7±0.2 g/m2s while the critical heat flux was 45±1 kW/m2." The companion paper, Emberley, Do, Yim, Torero, *FSJ* 91:252–258 ("Critical heat flux and mass loss rate for extinction…"), finds the values **depend on species**. Cuevas et al. (*CNF*, UCL manuscript, p.5) summarise it as "critical mass-loss rate … of six different types of timber ranged from 2.7 to 8.3 g/m2s, while the corresponding critical external incident heat fluxes ranged from 24 to 57 kW/m2". Emberley et al., *PCI* 36 (2017), "Self-extinction of timber", gives ~4.0 g/m²s. So 45/3.7 is one species and product. It is not a general timber constant. |
| 2a | MaCFP-2 PMMA cone: ~11 labs at 25 kW/m², ~10 at 65 | **CONFIRMED (current repo)** | I counted the cone CSVs in `PMMA/Calibration_Data`. At 25 kW/m² there are 11 labs: DBI_Lund, Edinburgh, GIDAZE+, HKPoly, LCPP, NIST, TIFP, UClan, UDRI, ULSTER, UQ (35 tests). At 65 kW/m² there are 10 labs: Aalto plus the same set without NIST and ULSTER. FM's files are FPA, not cone. Caveat: the 2021 report (`Documents/MaCFP_2021_Report_Part_I_Experimental.tex`, Table "Test Matrix of Cone Calorimeter Experiments") lists 11 institutions in total. Its text says "Ten institutions submitted a total of 30 cone calorimeter tests conducted at 25" and "Ten institutions … 27 tests … at 65". |
| 2b | MaCFP-4 pine: ~10 labs, 91 tests, 25–75 kW/m² | **CONFIRMED** | `MaCFP-4_Experimental_Summary.tex` §Cone says: "Ten institutions submitted experimental measurements from 91 unique cone calorimeter tests conducted using an applied external heat flux of 25 … 30 … 45 … 50 … 60 … and 75 kW/m²". It adds "70 tests … parallel … 21 … perpendicular". My file count is 10 labs and 92 CSVs. |
| 2c | Lab-mean PMMA t_ig at 25 kW/m² spans ~76–148 s | **CONFIRMED** | 2021 report, Table_12 (t_ign = first time Q″ ≥ 24 kW/m²): lab means at 25 kW/m² run from 76 ('Drummondville') to 148 ('Sherbrooke'). My recomputation from the current CSVs, with the same 24 kW/m² rule, gives 77 (HKPoly) to 147.5 s (UQ). **Caution:** the report flags the 76 s lab as a "clear outlier". Without it the spread is about 99–148 s. |
| 2d | Pine first-peak HRR at 50 kW/m² spans ~161–235 kW/m² | **CONFIRMED (by computation; the summary text has no such sentence)** | I computed lab means of the first-peak HRR, taken as the maximum within 200 s of t_ig, for parallel-grain tests. BUWfd 161.2, FPL 205.0, IMT 206.1, FSRI 235.0 (FSRI perpendicular grain: 237.7). Only **4 labs** tested at 50 kW/m². The summary tex has no 161/235 statement. The range depends on how "first peak" is defined, and FSRI's R3 alone peaks at 288. |
| 2e | ~46% of pine series meet ASTM E1354 t_ig repeatability | **CONFIRMED** | MaCFP-4 summary: "only 46.4% of experimental series satisfied the ASTM repeatability criterion for calculated ignition time, compared with 32.0% for reported ignition time and 43.5% for heat of combustion." |
| 3 | NIST TN 2102: FDS peak ~800/440/330 kW at 8/4/2 cm vs ~390 kW measured; residue ~5%; ~39 kg pine crib 56×56×46 cm, 38 mm sticks, glowing tail | **PARTLY. The source is mixed up and the FDS numbers are stale.** | (i) **TN 2102 contains no FDS work.** It is "Heat Release Rates of Multiple Transient Combustibles" (McGrattan, 2020), an experimental report. (ii) Crib facts, confirmed from TN 2102 §2: "Pine wood crib with dimensions 56 cm by 56 cm by 46 cm … slats with a cross-section 3.8 cm … approximately 39 kg … moisture content … less than 5 %". Test 13 peak HRR is **396 ± 18 kW**. Table 1 gives the crib residue yield as **0.053 ± 0.034** (Test 13 alone: 2.85/39.39 = 7.2%). The Test 13 note says "At approximately 48 min, the hot coals were spread out and raked into a water bath". That supports a coal or glowing phase, but the report never uses the word "glowing". (iii) The FDS comparison is in the **FDS Validation Guide**, §"NIST/NRC Transient Combustibles": "grid resolution … 8 cm, with additional simulations … 4 cm and 2 cm … for the single crib". I took peak HRR from `firemodels/out/NIST_NRC_Transient_Combustibles/crib_1x1x1_*cm_cat_hrr.csv`. The **2021 outputs (FDS 6.7.6)** give 798 / 437 / 334 kW, which **matches the claim**. The **current outputs (FDS-6.11.1-165)** give **485 / 392 / 389 kW**. So the claim describes a five-year-old FDS version. Current FDS converges near the measured value even at 4 cm. |
| 4 | Finney 2015 PNAS: fine fuels don't ignite from radiation alone; ignition needs flame contact (convection) | **CONFIRMED, with scope** | Finney et al. 2015, PMC4538634, Introduction: "Individual fine particles will not ignite solely from radiation even at the preignition fluxes reported for forest crown fires (<∼80 kW⋅m−2)". Abstract: "Convective cooling of the fine-sized fuel particles … efficiently offset heating by thermal radiation until convective heating by contact with flames and hot gasses occurs." Scope: this holds for *fine* particles (roughly ≤1 mm, high convective h) in wildland beds. It is not a general rule for thick fuels. |
| 5 | Jiang et al. 2019 dowel arrays: upward by flame contact, lateral by radiation; lateral spread at 0.75 cm, not at 1.5 cm; 13.6 s vs 19.8 s | **PARTLY. The timing is UNVERIFIABLE.** | Jiang et al., *PCI* 37:3767–3774. Abstract (via the Berkeley Fire Lab page): spacings 0.75, 0.875 and 1.5 cm. "Flames were found to spread vertically for all spacings; … for the 1.5 cm spacing, the gap was too large for horizontal flame spread to occur." Also: "A radiation-controlled model for horizontal flame spread was developed … Combined with an existing convection-based model for vertical flame spread". The mechanism and the gap statement are confirmed. Note that the paper says "convection-based", not "flame contact". I could not reach the full text (the NSF PAR copy timed out, ScienceDirect returned 403), so the 13.6 s / 19.8 s comparison is unverified. |
| 6 | LIFT / ASTM E1321 "Table 2" (Quintiere): no downward/lateral spread on cold plywood or PMMA; ~1.2–3.0 mm/s when preheated to 250–300 °C | **PARTLY. The velocities are derived, not tabulated.** | The table is Table 2, "Flame Spread Properties", in Quintiere & Harkleroad, NBSIR 84-2943 (1984), "New Concepts for Measuring Flame Spread Properties", p.28. That is the precursor of ASTM E1321. Its rows: Plywood, plain (1.27 cm) T_ig 390 °C, T_s,min 120 °C, Φ/kρc 24. PMMA Type G T_ig 378, T_s,min 90, Φ/kρc 14. PMMA Polycast T_ig 278, T_s,min 120, Φ/kρc 8. The table gives **no velocities**. Feeding plywood (1.27 cm) into V = (Φ/kρc)/(T_ig−T_s)² gives 24/140² = **1.2 mm/s** at 250 °C and 24/90² = **3.0 mm/s** at 300 °C, which is exactly the claimed range. PMMA Type G gives 0.85–2.3 mm/s. "No spread when cold" is consistent with T_s,min of 90–120 °C. But the report warns (p.21) that, because of the apparatus flux limits, "values of T_s,min < 80 °C could apply to much lower temperatures". |
| 7 | Treede 1995 J Physiol: C-fibre heat threshold median ~41 °C; type II AMH ~46 °C, ~120 ms latency, first pain | **CONFIRMED** | Treede, Meyer, Raja, Campbell, *J Physiol* 483:747–758, abstract: Type II AMHs had "a mean response latency of 120 ms, a median heat threshold of 46 degrees C". CMHs had "a mean response latency of 100 ms and a median heat threshold of 41 degrees C". And: "Only type II AMHs had response latencies that were short enough to explain first pain to heat." Scope: **monkey** hairy skin. Type II AMHs were absent in glabrous skin, and so was first pain to heat there. |
| 8 | NOAA/FEMA table: at 2 kW/m², pain ~45 s, second-degree burn ~187 s | **CONFIRMED** | NOAA ALOHA page "Thermal Radiation Levels of Concern", table "Time for physiological effects (on bare skin)…". The row for 2 kW/m² reads 45 s (labelled "Time for **Severe** Pain") and 187 s (2nd-degree burns). Source given there: FEMA/DOT/EPA 1988, *Handbook of Chemical Hazard Analysis Procedures*. The same page's ALOHA default LOC uses "2 kW/(sq m) (pain within 60 seconds)", so it is not internally consistent. |
| 9a | FDS Tech Ref: 1D solid conduction uses Crank–Nicolson + tridiagonal solve | **CONFIRMED** | Tech Ref, Appendices, §"One-Dimensional Heat Conduction in Solids": "updated in time using a Crank-Nicolson scheme"; "a tri-diagonal linear solver is used". |
| 9b | Solid substeps capped at 10 per gas step | **CONFIRMED, but the source is the User's Guide and there is nuance** | User's Guide (solid-phase tips): "`TIME_STEP_FACTOR`. Its default value is 10, meaning that the solid phase time step … can be sub-divided by *at most* a factor of 10", triggered when the internal ΔT exceeds `DELTA_TMP_MAX` (default 25 °C). The Tech Ref adds that by default the solid step is **2× the gas step** (`WALL_INCREMENT`), and that reductions go in powers of two: Δt = Δt_gas·2^−⌈ln η/ln 2⌉. |
| 9c | Minimum layer/cell sizes given in metres | **CONFIRMED** | User's Guide: `MINIMUM_LAYER_THICKNESS` default is "1e-6 m or 0.1 times the specified THICKNESS, whichever is less". `CELL_SIZE` is in metres, e.g. "CELL_SIZE=0.003". `N_LAYER_CELLS_MAX` defaults to 1000. |
| 9d | D* = (Q/(ρ c_p T √g))^(2/5) | **CONFIRMED** | User's Guide §mesh resolution, which writes it with ρ_∞ and T_∞. |
| 9e | Validation range of D*/dx ≈ 3–19 | **WRONG** | The Validation Guide table "Summary of important numerical parameters" lists D*/δx values from **~1 (1–3) up to 118 (20–118)**. The User's Guide explicitly says: "These values range over two orders of magnitude … Taking any of these values as an 'acceptable' minimum is inappropriate." A "3–19" window does not appear as a validated range. |
| 10 | Murray et al. 2014 failed to replicate Fessler's claim that childhood fire experience ends fire fascination | **CONFIRMED in substance; the framing is off** | Murray, Fessler, Lupfer, "Young flames…", *Evol. Behav. Sci.* (2014). **Fessler is a co-author.** The paper *tests* Fessler's "Exposure Hypothesis", which rested on ethnographic reports; it does not re-run a prior empirical study. Abstract: "Study 1 found no significant association … Study 2 found that, contrary to the Exposure Hypothesis, more frequent exposure to fire in childhood was associated with more positive psychological associations with fire." |
| 11a | Apparent heat of gasification of wood from cone data ~14.6 kJ/g vs intrinsic 1.6–2.6 kJ/g | **UNVERIFIABLE as cited; the order of magnitude is plausible** | I found no source for "14.6" (searched "14.6 kJ/g", the Janssens 1993 Interflam record and the MaCFP tex). As my own check, I regressed the mean MLR from t_ig+60 to t_ig+600 s against flux over MaCFP-4 parallel-grain cone tests (25–75 kW/m², 67 tests): slope 0.064 g/m²s per kW/m², so **apparent L ≈ 15.7 kJ/g**. That is the same order as 14.6. I did not verify the intrinsic 1.6–2.6 kJ/g. |
| 11b | MaCFP-4 fitted char conductivity 0.15–0.30 W/m·K | **PARTLY / too narrow** | From `Wood/Material_Properties/*.json`: UCB-CONST-1 char 0.148–0.299 W/m·K. UCB-CONST-2/3 char 0.10–0.12 (parallel), 0.30 (perpendicular). UCB-TDEP k0 0.084–0.124 rising with T. UMD_FSRI-OPT char 0.270 (inverse fit). CORIA char **0.08**. UMD_FSRI-DM uses *measured* LFA values of 0.04–0.09 for all components. The fitted values therefore span **~0.08–0.30**, and are anisotropic in the UCB sets. 0.15–0.30 is a subset. |
| 12 | Lyon & Quintiere 2007, *Combust. Flame* 151:551–559: 24 kW/m² vs 66 ± 17 kW/m² | **PARTLY. The two numbers do not conflict.** | I could not get the full text or the verbatim abstract (ScienceDirect, Academia and ResearchGate returned 403; ADS and OSTI were unreachable; Crossref and S2 have the abstract elided). Search-engine extracts of the ADS/OSTI abstract and several citing sources put it this way: **~24 kW/m²** is the HRR at *incipient* (flash-point) piloted ignition, paired with a critical mass flux of ~1 g/m²s and a gas energy density of ~1.9 MJ/m³ (≈ the lower flammability limit). **~66 kW/m²** (quoted by citers as 66 ± 17) is the *sustained* (fire-point) critical HRR. The 24 kW/m² use is confirmed from primary text in the MaCFP-2 report: "t_ign … defined as the time at which Q″ ≥ 24 kW/m² [lyon2007criteria]". The MaCFP-4 scripts use the same rule. Read the paper itself before relying on the ±17. |

## Points that matter for the design

1. **Claim 1:** do not treat 45 kW/m² / 3.7 g/m²s as a universal timber constant. It is Radiata Pine CLT in one rig. The companion study gives 24–57 kW/m² and 2.7–8.3 g/m²s across species. In MaCFP-4 itself, the 25 kW/m² pine cone tests settle at a mean MLR of about 3.8 g/m²s and still burn, which shows the threshold depends on the setup.
2. **Claim 3:** the "FDS overpredicts 2× at 8 cm" story describes FDS 6.7.6 (2021). With current FDS 6.11 the overshoot at 8 cm is about 1.2× (485 vs 396 kW), and 4 cm and 2 cm land within 1% of the measurement. Cite the Validation Guide and give the FDS version.
3. **Claim 9e:** no validated D*/dx window exists. FDS's own guide warns against reading one off the table.
4. **Claim 2c:** the low end of 76 s is a flagged outlier lab.
